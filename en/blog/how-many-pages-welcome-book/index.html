How many pages for a welcome book?

Too short and it doesn't answer questions. Too long and nobody reads it. There's no magic number, but there is a good way to think about length.

By Gustave5 min readUpdated 24 October 2026

The rule: one page, one question

Each page should answer a question a guest has: how do I connect, where do we eat tonight, when do the bins go out. A page that answers nothing can go.

Rough sizes

  • A city studio: 8 to 12 pages is often enough.
  • A house with a garden: 15 to 25 pages.
  • A villa with a pool in an area full of sights: 30 pages or more, as long as there's an index.

The book on the home page has 39 pages because the house is close to many places to visit. The ten-chapter index keeps it easy to use.

Where the space goes

In most books, places (sights, beaches, restaurants, shops) take more than half the pages. Practical information and rules fit in a few well-organised pages.

If a chapter goes beyond five or six pages, split it with clear headings. Nobody reads a welcome book cover to cover: they look things up.

How many words per page

A welcome book page is read in seconds. Aim for paragraphs of two to four lines, lists rather than blocks of text, and one clear heading per topic. A page that's too dense is a page people skip.

The ten-second test

Open your book at random. In ten seconds, do you know what the page is about and where the key information is? If not, shorten it or add subheadings.

What to cut

  • The full history of the region, copied from a guide.
  • Places you don't know yourself.
  • Rules repeated in several places.
  • Instructions for obvious appliances.

Frequently asked questions

Is a long book a problem?

Only if it's badly organised. With an index and clear chapters, a 40-page book is still easy to use.

Should pages be numbered?

Yes, as soon as the book has an index. That's how guests find the right page in seconds.

Should the phone version be as long?

It can hold the same, organised in sections opened one at a time. Length matters much less on screen.
