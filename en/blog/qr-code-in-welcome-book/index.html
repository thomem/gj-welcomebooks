A QR code in your welcome book

A small square printed on the cover, and the paper book becomes a pocket book too. Used well, the QR code links the two.

By Gustave5 min readUpdated 24 October 2026

What it's for

  • Opening the phone version of the book, to take on outings.
  • Finding the map of places with directions from the house.
  • Choosing a language when the printed book is in only one.

Where to put it

On the cover, at the bottom, with a line saying what it opens: “Scan for the phone version”. A QR code with no explanation rarely gets scanned. You can repeat it on the places page.

Size and contrast

Keep it large enough to scan easily, dark on a light background, with a white margin around it. Avoid placing it on a photo or busy pattern.

Test it with two different phones, in evening light, before printing all your copies.

The trap to avoid

If the link behind the QR code changes or stops working, every printed book becomes wrong. You need a link that stays the same over time. That's one reason for the yearly update: the phone version stays hosted and the QR code keeps working.

What a QR code must not contain

The key box code, the alarm code or anything that gives access to the house. The book stays on site and can be photographed: that information goes by message, at the right time.

Testing it properly

  • Scan with an iPhone and an Android phone.
  • Scan in evening light, not just in daylight.
  • Check the page loads fast, even on a slow connection.
  • Check the page is in the right language or offers a choice.

Wi‑Fi by QR code?

It's possible and handy, but keep the password written out too: not every phone handles it the same way, and some guests travel with a laptop.

Frequently asked questions

Do guests really scan QR codes?

Yes, when they know what they'll find. A clear line next to the code makes all the difference.

Do you need an app to read a QR code?

No, the camera on most recent phones reads it directly.

What if I change website?

Printed QR codes would point to a page that no longer exists. That's why you need a link that stays stable over time, like the one included in the yearly update.
